Who We Are

Pakistan's National Carrier

Pakistan International Airlines is the flag carrier of Pakistan, headquartered at Jinnah International Airport in Karachi. Since 1946, PIA has connected the people of Pakistan to each other and to the world.

Airline websites play a critical role in the passenger experience. They must support trust, enable clear decision-making, and guide users through booking without confusion — whether the user is a first-time traveller or a seasoned passenger.

HCI Rationale

Why Redesign Matters

Airline websites are among the most complex consumer-facing products. They handle high-stakes decisions — money, travel plans, schedules — often under time pressure. Poor usability directly leads to booking errors, missed flights, and lost trust.

This prototype applies five HCI principles — accessibility, consistency, navigation, user feedback, and efficiency — to redesign the core passenger journey with a focus on usability, clarity, and inclusive design.

🧠
Reduce Confusion

Complex menus and scattered services create decision fatigue. Redesigned navigation groups related tasks logically and uses clear labels so passengers always know where they are.

🚫
Prevent Booking Mistakes

Smart form validation, city autocomplete, same-city prevention, and date constraints stop errors before they happen — rather than punishing users after submission.

🔍
Make Support Easy to Find

Special assistance, refund requests, and contact options are placed where users actually need them — prominently visible, not buried in sub-menus or footnotes.

💬
Provide Clear Feedback

Every action gets a response: loading states, success toasts, inline validation, empty states. Passengers always know the status of their request — reducing anxiety and support calls.

♿
Work for All Passengers

High contrast mode, adjustable font size, reduced motion, and visible skip links ensure the redesign works for users with visual, motor, or cognitive differences — not just the majority.

🎓

Academic Prototype Note

This project is an academic HCI redesign prototype submitted for a Human Computer Interaction course. It uses mock flight data and focuses entirely on usability, interaction design, and passenger task completion. It does not represent real airline operations, real payment processing, or real booking confirmations.
